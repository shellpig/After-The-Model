A 2D side-scrolling video game background. STRICTLY orthographic
side view, like Hollow Knight, The Last Night, Limbo, or Ranx (1990).
Matching the visual style of anchor-01, anchor-02, anchor-03 in
/assets/art_bible/.

## CANVAS
- Aspect ratio ~16:3 (3840 x 720), a WIDE horizontal panorama,
  THREE screens wide. Designed to be traversed LEFT to RIGHT.
- The walking floor is a SINGLE continuous horizontal line across
  the ENTIRE 3840 width — perfectly level end to end, no steps,
  no ramps in the walkable strip. The platform floor runs UNBROKEN
  all the way to the FAR RIGHT EDGE of the frame.

## SCENE — Abandoned, OUT-OF-SERVICE subway PLATFORM
A decommissioned subway platform. Service has STOPPED — dead
departure screens, no trains, no people — BUT the power is NOT
fully cut: residual emergency lighting still flickers, a few
indicators glow weakly. "Half-derelict, not a pitch-black ruin."
This is the 下沉門檻 (the sinking threshold): the commuter world
above is dead, the only warmth leaks UP from the settlement below.

## SCENE FRAMING
The platform INTERIOR fills the ENTIRE frame. Floor, tiled back
wall, and ceiling extend to all edges. NO external structure, NO
sky, NO "outside the station." Orthographic — no perspective, no
vanishing point, floor and ceiling are perfectly horizontal lines.

## THE TRACK (CRITICAL — front edge, runs full width)
Along the FRONT bottom edge of the platform, for the ENTIRE width
of the frame (left edge to right edge), runs the train track: the
platform lip with a faded yellow tactile warning strip, and below
it a DARK recessed track trench (empty — no train, no rail detail,
just a shadowed void where trains used to run). This track runs
the WHOLE length and NEVER stops — the platform never turns into
a staircase, because trains run along this front edge end to end.
The trench is visual only, NOT walkable; the player walks on the
platform strip above and behind it.

## HORIZONTAL LAYOUT (three thirds — keep them distinct)

- LEFT THIRD (~0-33%): a plain tiled subway wall. On the FAR LEFT,
  ONE dark open DOORWAY / archway passage cut into the wall,
  leading off-frame to the left (a quiet dark opening, dim, no
  machinery inside). Beside or above it, ONE faded EXIT sign with
  a LEFT-pointing arrow. The platform floor in this third is
  completely EMPTY and CLEAR — nothing stands on it, no machines,
  no barriers, no objects, just worn tile floor and the tiled wall.
  Calm, institutional, empty.

- CENTER THIRD (~33-66%): the MAIN PLATFORM BODY — the breathing
  space. Place the dead COMMUTE / DEPARTURE SCREEN and the bench
  TOGETHER, HORIZONTALLY CENTERED at the middle of the full frame
  (~50% width): the large screen mounted high on the back wall,
  the worn bench directly BELOW it. The screen is a stopped board
  — black with faint flickering static or a single frozen frame,
  NO schedule, NO readable text; it is the visual focal prop and a
  future story anchor. On the platform floor JUST BESIDE the bench:
  ONE old, worn, hard-shell SUITCASE left standing (or slightly
  fallen over), as if someone forgot to take it — abandoned, dusty,
  muted desaturated color (NOT orange, NOT bright). It quietly
  implies "someone was here and is gone." Around the center: water
  stains, hairline wall cracks, exposed pipes along the ceiling
  edge with slow water DRIPS.

- RIGHT THIRD (~66-100%): the platform CONTINUES, walkable, all the
  way to the far right edge — tiled wall, more pipes, decay. The
  DOWN-PASSAGE to the settlement sits at the VERY FAR RIGHT EDGE /
  bottom-right CORNER of the frame: show ONLY the TOP TIP of a
  stopped escalator — the first 2-3 steps + a short section of
  handrail beginning to descend DOWN into a floor opening, the rest
  sinking OFF the bottom-right CORNER of the frame. It is a small
  INTERACTION PROP set into the floor at the rightmost edge, NOT a
  big structure, NOT dominant — like the escalator hint in
  anchor-03 but going DOWN instead of up, and positioned in the
  far corner. The platform floor and the front track reach all the
  way to this right-edge opening. From the opening, a faint amber
  warm glow is FAINTLY visible rising from below (interior-comfort
  warm — "the settlement is down there," the only warmth, NOT the
  focal orange). DO NOT draw the escalator ascending.

## VERTICAL LAYOUT (CRITICAL)
- BOTTOM 15-20% of frame: the walking floor (thin horizontal strip),
  with the track trench just in front of/below it.
- REMAINING 80-85%: tiled back wall, ceiling, dead screen, pipes,
  signage — all NON-PLAYABLE visual context.
- The player must NEVER feel they can move up or down in the floor
  area. Floor is a thin strip for left-right walking only. DO NOT
  let the floor exceed 20% of frame height.

## SCALE REFERENCE
A standing adult human would be ~22-25% of frame height. The dead
departure screen is roughly head-to-chest height of a person.
Bench is knee-to-waist height. The forgotten suitcase is about
knee-high. The descending escalator hint is SMALL — only its top
tip shows, tucked into the far right corner.

## VIEW & FLAT PROFILE
- ORTHOGRAPHIC PROJECTION ONLY. No perspective. No vanishing point.
- Every object is a CARDBOARD CUTOUT against the back wall — flat
  side profile. The escalator hint shown in pure side profile, NO
  receding 3/4 stairs. Back wall is a flat backdrop.
- Floor stays CLEAR for a continuous horizontal walking path to the
  right edge.

## POWER STATE — "stopped but not dead" (read carefully)
- Departure screen: DEAD (black / frozen static flicker), no
  schedule. This is "the line going up has stopped."
- Ceiling lights: mostly OFF; ONE or TWO pale cyan-white emergency
  strips still flicker weakly.
- A couple of small indicator LEDs (cyan/magenta) glow faintly —
  residual power.
- NO trains. NO NPCs. NO active advertising. NO bright clean glow.

## ★ ORANGE SCALE RULE (CRITICAL) ★
Saturated burnt orange is the NARRATIVE MARKER COLOR of the
plebeian district — the riders' mark on a system that forgot them.
It must always be a SMALL ACCENT SHAPE, never a fill.
HARD LIMITS:
- TOTAL orange ≤ 1-2% of frame pixels. Not 5%, not 10%.
- Maximum TWO orange spots:
  (1) [PRIMARY] ONE small hand-marked orange arrow on the wall
      near the far-right down-passage, pointing DOWN toward the
      descending escalator hint — a rider's scrawl, "go down here."
  (2) [SUPPORTING] ONE small burnt-orange graffiti tag or sticker
      on the platform wall near the dead screen.
- Orange is ALWAYS a SHAPE (tag, stripe, glyph, arrow, sticker),
  NEVER a background fill, NEVER on official signage, the screen,
  or the suitcase.
- DO NOT confuse the warm amber down-glow with orange — that amber
  is faded interior comfort, NOT the saturated focal orange.

## COMPOSITION DISCIPLINE
- ~45% deep shadow (ceiling, dim corners, the dark track trench,
  unlit wall sections).
- ~40% midtones (tiled wall, floor).
- ~15% bright: the flickering emergency strip(s), faint cyan/magenta
  indicators, the faint warm amber leak from the down-passage, and
  the ~1-2% orange accents. Most of the frame is QUIET, COLD, EMPTY.

## STRICT DO NOT
- DO NOT draw any turnstiles, ticket barriers, fare gates, or
  waist-high gate machines ANYWHERE in the frame — the left side is
  just an empty tiled wall with a dark doorway and an EXIT sign.
- DO NOT turn the right side into a big staircase or let the
  escalator replace/cut off the platform — the platform floor and
  the front track BOTH run unbroken to the far right edge, where
  the small escalator tip descends in the corner.
- DO NOT draw the escalator ascending / going up; only a SMALL
  top-tip hint descending off the bottom-right corner.
- DO NOT make the suitcase orange or brightly colored — it is
  muted and abandoned-looking.
- DO NOT add trains, NPCs, or any living figures.
- DO NOT show a working/scrolling departure schedule or readable
  text on the screen — the screen is dead.
- DO NOT make the floor more than 20% of frame height.
- DO NOT use perspective lines or 3/4 views; no receding stairs.
- DO NOT make it look futuristic-clean, brightly lit, or in-service.
- DO NOT cover the walls with posters/graffiti (one dead screen +
  one bench + one suitcase + sparse pipes + max two small orange
  marks).
- DO NOT let orange exceed ~2% or spread beyond two spots.
- DO NOT use pure white (lightest = faded cream) or pure black
  (darkest = deep purple-black).
- DO NOT use gold or brass tones (that's the wealthy district).
- DO NOT use saturated cyber-UV-violet (that's the AI district).

## COMIC TECHNIQUE
American indie cyberpunk comic (HD 2D, NOT pixel art). Mike Mignola
heavy black for ceiling, track trench, and dim corners. Frank
Miller contrast. Paul Pope linework. Hand-inked varied-weight
lines. Halftone dots in midtones only. Flat cel-shade, no gradients.

## COLOR
Overall: deep teal, gray-blue, ink purple-black — cool,
institutional, underground, abandoned. Residual neon: faint cyan
and magenta indicator glints. Emergency light: pale cyan-white,
flickering. ONE faint warm amber glow rising from the down-passage
opening in the far right corner (interior-comfort warm, the only
warmth, signaling "life is below"). Signature accent: SATURATED
BURNT ORANGE — one rider's DOWN-arrow near the far-right passage
[primary] + one small graffiti tag near the dead screen
[supporting], ≤2% of frame total. No pure white, no pure black, no
gold/brass, no saturated UV-violet.
