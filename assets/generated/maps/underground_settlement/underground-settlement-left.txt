A 2D side-scrolling video game background. STRICTLY orthographic
side view, like Hollow Knight, The Last Night, Limbo, or Ranx (1990).
Matching the visual style of anchor-01, anchor-02, anchor-03 in
/assets/art_bible/.

## CANVAS
- Aspect ratio ~4.4:1 (3200 x 720), a wide horizontal panel, about
  2.5 screens wide. This is the LEFT HALF of a larger settlement
  map; it must tile seamlessly on its RIGHT edge to a second panel.
- The walking floor is a SINGLE continuous horizontal line across
  the ENTIRE width, level end to end. The floor height and the
  tunnel ceiling height at the RIGHT edge must stay consistent so
  the panel stitches cleanly to the next one.

## SCENE — Underground homeless SETTLEMENT (warm "home" side)
A storm-drain / maintenance tunnel repurposed into a shanty
settlement for people the system deleted — the "black-registry"
underclass who live below the dead subway. This LEFT panel is the
WARM, lived-in side: a cluster of tents under strings of warm
bulbs, signs of daily life. Theme = WARM HUMANITY vs COLD
INFRASTRUCTURE: cold concrete tunnel and pipes, warmed by people
who built a home in it. Battered but it has body heat.
IMPORTANT: NO people, NO figures, NO NPCs in frame — the tents are
EMPTY but full of TRACES of life (someone lives here, just stepped
away). Show the lived-in SPACE, not the inhabitants.

## SCENE FRAMING
The tunnel INTERIOR fills the ENTIRE frame. Floor, low concrete
tunnel ceiling, and back wall extend to all edges. NO sky, NO
"outside." Orthographic — no perspective, no vanishing point;
floor and ceiling read as horizontal. The tunnel is a converted
drainage/maintenance bore: a slightly arched concrete ceiling
suggested but kept flat/orthographic, exposed pipes and cable runs
along it.

## HORIZONTAL LAYOUT

- FAR LEFT (~0-18%): the DOWN-ESCALATOR you ARRIVE on — the way
  down from the dead subway above. Show ONLY the BOTTOM TIP of a
  STOPPED ESCALATOR: the last 2-3 steps where it meets the
  settlement floor, plus a short section of the diagonal handrail
  and side balustrade beginning to RISE UP-AND-LEFT and exiting the
  TOP-LEFT of the frame. It is a MECHANICAL escalator in pure side
  profile (flat repeating metal step pattern, solid side panel),
  NOT a plain stone staircase, NOT receding into depth — the same
  stopped escalator seen descending in the subway platform above,
  now seen from its bottom. From above-left spills a DIM, COOL,
  dead-blue light (the abandoned station overhead — cold, lifeless).
  This is where the player descends INTO the settlement: the COLD
  threshold between the dead world above and the warm home here.

- CENTER + RIGHT (~18-100%): the TENT CLUSTER — the settlement
  proper. Several improvised tents/shelters built from tarps,
  plastic sheeting, scavenged panels and crates, strung along the
  tunnel wall. Overhead: strings of warm amber bulbs (faded amber
  interior-comfort warm) zig-zag across the tunnel, the main source
  of warmth and light. The tents are CLOSED or half-open and EMPTY
  — no people — but surrounded by TRACES of life: a folding stool,
  a hung blanket or laundry line, a battered pot or kettle, stacked
  crates, a crate used as a table with a chipped mug, a child's
  small chalk drawing on the concrete. Personal, cramped, human.
  Along the tunnel: exposed pipes, conduit, a shallow dry drainage
  channel running low along the FRONT edge (visual only, the player
  walks on the walkway behind/above it). The RIGHT edge is an OPEN
  continuation of the tunnel (it leads to the rest of the
  settlement) — NOT a capped wall, so it stitches to the next panel.

## VERTICAL LAYOUT (CRITICAL)
- BOTTOM 15-20% of frame: the walking floor (thin horizontal strip
  / walkway), with the shallow drainage channel just in front of it.
- REMAINING 80-85%: tunnel wall, low ceiling, pipes, tents, strung
  bulbs — all NON-PLAYABLE visual context.
- The player must NEVER feel they can move up or down in the floor
  area. Floor is a thin strip for left-right walking only. DO NOT
  let the floor exceed 20% of frame height.

## SCALE REFERENCE
A standing adult human would be ~22-25% of frame height. Tents are
about human height or lower (cramped). Bulbs, stools, crates are
small human-scaled props. The escalator bottom-tip is a small
corner prop, only its base showing.

## VIEW & FLAT PROFILE
- ORTHOGRAPHIC PROJECTION ONLY. No perspective. No vanishing point.
- Every object is a CARDBOARD CUTOUT against the tunnel wall — flat
  side profile. The escalator shown in pure side hint, NO receding
  3/4 stairs. Back wall is a flat backdrop.
- Floor stays CLEAR for a continuous horizontal walking path.

## "TRACES NOT PEOPLE" (Phase requirement — read carefully)
- NO human figures, NO NPCs, NO silhouettes of people anywhere.
- The tents are empty. Convey habitation ONLY through objects and
  warm light. This is "real emptiness with signs someone lives
  here," not an abandoned ruin and not a populated camp.
- Do NOT pre-place specialized workstations or named-character
  setups — just generic, humble dwelling clutter.

## ★ ORANGE SCALE RULE (CRITICAL) ★
Saturated burnt orange is the NARRATIVE MARKER COLOR of the
plebeian / deleted underclass — "human will refusing to be
forgotten." It must always be a SMALL ACCENT SHAPE, never a fill.
HARD LIMITS:
- TOTAL orange ≤ 1-2% of frame pixels. Not 5%, not 10%.
- Maximum TWO orange spots:
  (1) [PRIMARY] ONE small burnt-orange mark made by a resident — a
      child's chalk drawing, a hand-painted symbol, or a small
      tag on the concrete tunnel wall near the tents.
  (2) [SUPPORTING] ONE small orange detail on a personal object —
      a stripe on a crate, a small cloth, a sticker.
- Orange is ALWAYS a SHAPE, NEVER a background fill, NEVER on the
  pipes or structural concrete.
- DO NOT confuse the warm amber bulb glow with the focal orange —
  the amber bulbs are faded interior-comfort warmth (large soft
  glow), the orange is a tiny saturated narrative SHAPE.

## COMPOSITION DISCIPLINE
- ~45% deep shadow (tunnel ceiling, dim corners, the cold
  escalator threshold, unlit recesses).
- ~40% midtones (concrete walls, floor, tents).
- ~15% bright: the warm amber bulb strings (the heart of the
  frame), a few small cool highlights, and the ~1-2% orange accents.
- Contrast the COLD blue escalator threshold (left) against the
  WARM amber tent cluster (center-right). Most of the frame is dim,
  intimate, cramped.

## STRICT DO NOT
- DO NOT draw any people, figures, NPCs, or human silhouettes.
- DO NOT draw a plain stone/concrete STAIRCASE on the left — it is
  a MECHANICAL stopped ESCALATOR (flat repeating metal steps, side
  balustrade), the same one descending from the subway above; show
  only its bottom tip rising up-and-left off frame.
- DO NOT cap the RIGHT edge with a wall — the tunnel continues open
  to the right so it can stitch to the next panel.
- DO NOT make it look like a clean campground or a bright shelter —
  it is a cramped repurposed drain tunnel, humble and worn.
- DO NOT make the floor more than 20% of frame height.
- DO NOT use perspective lines or 3/4 views; no receding stairs.
- DO NOT add readable text — any marks are abstract shapes/symbols.
- DO NOT let orange exceed ~2% or spread beyond two spots.
- DO NOT use pure white (lightest = faded cream) or pure black
  (darkest = deep purple-black).
- DO NOT use gold or brass tones (that's the wealthy district).
- DO NOT use saturated cyber-UV-violet (that's the AI district).

## COMIC TECHNIQUE
American indie cyberpunk comic (HD 2D, NOT pixel art). Mike Mignola
heavy black for tunnel ceiling and dim corners. Frank Miller
contrast. Paul Pope linework. Hand-inked varied-weight lines.
Halftone dots in midtones only. Flat cel-shade, no gradients.

## COLOR
Overall base: deep teal, gray-blue, ink purple-black — cold,
underground concrete. The COLD accent (left escalator threshold):
dim dead-blue light spilling from the abandoned station above. The
WARM accent (center-right): faded amber bulb strings, the home's
body heat — the dominant emotional warmth of the frame (interior-
comfort warm, soft and large, NOT the focal orange). Signature
accent: SATURATED BURNT ORANGE — one resident's chalk mark/symbol
[primary] + one small orange detail on a personal object
[supporting], ≤2% of frame total. No pure white, no pure black, no
gold/brass, no saturated UV-violet.
